The AI Prospect-Prep System
Walk in knowing more than they expect.

Four prompts. Ten minutes of prep. Whether it's a kitchen table or a recruiting coffee — show up already understanding their world better than anyone else who's pitched them.

AI won't replace financial professionals. The advisor who preps every case with it will replace the one who wings it.

Client mode: prepping for a working family, healthcare professional, or military household. Goal — map their gaps and close in their language.

!

Turn on Deep Research first. In ChatGPT, tap the prompt bar and select Deep Research before sending. Without it you get a shallow answer. Run all four prompts back-to-back, set the phone down ~10 minutes, then ask it to organize the output. Use the $20/mo Plus plan.

1

Needs Profile

Map the gaps

Find what a household like theirs almost always overlooks — before you ever sit down.

Act as a financial-needs analyst. I'm meeting a prospective client and want to understand their likely protection and retirement gaps before our appointment.

Household details:
- Profession / income range: [e.g. perioperative RN, ~$120k]
- Age & life stage: [e.g. 41, married]
- Dependents: [e.g. 3 kids, ages 8–15]
- Existing coverage / savings: [e.g. employer term only, small 401k]
- Stated concern: [e.g. "what happens to the kids if something happens to me"]

Map the protection and retirement gaps a household in this exact situation typically faces. Highlight what families like this most often overlook — including long-term care exposure and income protection. Give a short summary I can use to prepare, not a sales pitch.
2

Solution Comparison

Prep only — see guardrail

A plain-language side-by-side, framed in tradeoffs a non-finance person understands.

Build a plain-language, side-by-side comparison of the vehicles that address this gap: [e.g. term + LTC hybrid vs. IUL vs. FIA].

For a [profession / age / family situation] who cares most about [e.g. protecting income + leaving a legacy], explain each option in terms of: what it does, who it fits, the main tradeoff, and how it's funded.

Write it so a non-finance person understands it. This is for my own preparation — do not produce numbers, projections, or illustrations. Keep it conceptual.
3

Objection Map

Anticipate the pushback

Know what they'll say before they say it — then layer your D.E.T.E. and NEPQ responses on top.

List the top 6 objections a [e.g. nurse / military spouse / small-business owner] typically raises when discussing life insurance and retirement planning.

For each objection, give: (1) the real concern underneath it, and (2) an empathetic, question-based reframe that keeps the conversation open rather than defensive. Avoid pushy or scripted-sounding language.
4

Personalized Framing

The one that closes

Tie the recommendation to their values — legacy, faith, "no family left behind." This is the language that closes, not the numbers.

Help me frame my recommendation around what this family actually values, not the product features.

What matters to them: [e.g. faith, providing for kids, never being a burden, building a legacy].
The core recommendation: [e.g. LTC-protected coverage + a vehicle for tax-advantaged retirement income].

Write 3 short, warm talking points that connect this recommendation to those values in plain, human language. No jargon. It should sound like a trusted advisor who genuinely cares, not a salesperson.
1

Fit Profile

Why this person, why this opportunity

Understand how the GX opportunity maps to their current job and life before the coffee.

Act as a recruiting strategist. I'm meeting a potential business partner about an opportunity in financial services (the WFG / GX path). Help me understand how to position it for them specifically.

Their background:
- Current job / field: [e.g. bedside RN, 12-hr shifts]
- Life stage & motivation: [e.g. 38, two kids, feeling capped at work]
- What they've expressed wanting: [e.g. more time, more income, purpose]

Map how a part-time financial services opportunity realistically fits someone in this exact situation — the transferable skills they already have, what would excite them, and what would worry them. Give me a prep summary, not a pitch.
2

Income & Time Math

Make it concrete

Turn "extra income" into something they can picture against their actual life.

Help me build a simple, honest way to explain part-time vs. full-time potential in a way that's concrete for this person — without making income guarantees or unrealistic claims.

Their situation: [e.g. works full-time, has ~8 free hours/week].

Show me how to frame the math of consistent part-time effort over time using conservative, illustrative language. Emphasize activity and skill-building over promises. Flag anything that would be a compliance risk to say out loud.
3

Objection Map

The WFG hesitations

Get ahead of the "is this one of those things?" reflex with honest, grounded answers.

List the top 6 objections or hesitations a thoughtful person typically raises when first hearing about a part-time financial services / WFG-style opportunity (e.g. "is this MLM," "I don't have a finance background," "no time," "I don't want to sell to friends").

For each, give: (1) the genuine concern underneath it, and (2) an honest, non-defensive reframe that respects their intelligence. No hype.
4

Personalized "Why Now"

The one that recruits

Connect the opportunity to who they are — purpose, family, the mission of leaving no family behind.

Help me frame this opportunity around what this person actually cares about, not the comp plan.

What drives them: [e.g. faith, providing for family, helping their community, more freedom].
The core invitation: [e.g. learn this, build part-time, serve families who've been overlooked].

Write 3 short, warm talking points connecting this opportunity to those values — the "No Family Left Behind" mission of serving working and underserved families. Make it sound like a sincere invitation from a mentor, not a recruiter.
From prompts to a deck

The presentation pipeline

Once the four prompts have run, turn the research into a custom one-pager you can send an hour after the meeting.

1

Organize it

Tell ChatGPT: "Organize all of this into a clear outline I can paste into Gamma."

2

Build the deck

Paste into gamma.app (free) — or Manus for the prettier version. Choose "preserve text as-is."

3

Export & send

Export to PDF, send it personally. This is how a custom presentation lands an hour after you met them — while you're still top of mind.

The 30-second video text

When a recruit or prospect goes cold, a short selfie video text converts where another email never will. Costs nothing but a minute. Hold the phone up, keep it casual, no pressure.

"Hey [name], it's Glenn — no pressure at all, just wanted to put a face to the name and say I'm here whenever you're ready. Shoot me a text back if anything comes up."
Non-negotiable

! The compliance guardrail

This is our world, not a real estate trainer's. AI is for prep only. The line between research and a client-facing number is bright — never cross it.

  • Any AI-generated comparison or income figure is preparation only. It never goes to a client as-is.
  • Client-facing numbers come from actual carrier illustrations — NASHP, ForeIncome II, Athene, Nationwide, Global Atlantic — never from ChatGPT.
  • Every projection and recommendation passes WFG / RFM suitability and compliance review before it reaches the client.
  • Never present an AI-built projection as if it were an illustration. Triple-check beats trust.
  • Recruiting: no income guarantees, no unrealistic earnings claims — illustrative and conservative only.
