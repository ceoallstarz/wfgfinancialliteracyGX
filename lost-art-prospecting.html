Hyperion Legacy Builders · Field Guide 01

The lost art:
walk in cold,
walk out with momentum.

Anybody can build off a phone. This is the skill that means you could be dropped in a city with no leads, no list, no following — and still build. Four stages: get your head right, use your eyes, open your mouth, get the contact.

Rooted. Rising. Unstoppable.

Before anything else

This is CEO work

A $30-an-hour employee waits to be handed people. A CEO goes and finds talent. Same person, same city, same Starbucks — different job description in their own head. That's the whole difference, and it shows up in your posture before you say a word.

You are not out here looking for bodies. You are out here recognizing talent.

The only two wins that count

Did you have the conversation, and did you leave with a way to reach them. That's it. Nobody is graded on who signs, who shows, or who buys.

Volume beats talent

The person who talks to the most people wins every time. You don't need to be smooth. You need reps, and reps hand you confidence for free.

You're collecting people

Every person you meet is a door. Some are locked today and open in two years. You're not closing anybody — you're building a list of humans who like you.

Recruiting sharpens the leader

Every conversation trains your radar for what greatness looks like. You can't lead people you never learned how to see.

Stage 1

Mindset: five seconds is the whole ask

You don't need to be brave all day. You need to be brave long enough to open your mouth. After the first sentence, it's just two people talking.

Name the fear honestly

The fear isn't them. The fear is you — worried about looking stupid, worried what a stranger thinks of you for nine seconds. That's ego, and ego is the most selfish thing you can bring into a room. Flip the camera around: this one conversation could be the day somebody's life changed and they had no idea it was available. If you stay quiet, they never find out.

Fear is ego. Ego points at you. Courage points at them.

The pre-approach ritual — run it every single time

  1. Body firstShoulders back, chin up, hands out of pockets, phone away. Your body sets your state, not the other way around.
  2. Pick oneChoose the actual human before you start walking. "That lady at the counter." Vague intention dies at the door.
  3. Smile before you speakSmile a half second early. It changes your voice, and it tells them you're safe before they hear a word.
  4. Say the line in your head"They already like me — they just don't know me yet." Every friend you have started as a stranger.
  5. Go on fiveDon't negotiate with yourself. Five seconds, then feet move.

The 5-Second Courage Button

Standing in the parking lot talking yourself out of it? Hit it, walk on zero.

5
Pick your person first.

Your baseline energy is the real script

Baseline energy is your default setting when nobody's watching — how you walk into a building alone. Slouched and scrolling reads as low confidence and low standards. Walk in like you own the place, even when you're by yourself, and 85% of the communication is done before you talk. Confident, not arrogant. Eye contact, not staring. Warm, not needy.

Stage 2

Eyes: it's not what you say, it's what you see

Scripts are the easy part. The skill is recognition — spotting the one person in the room who is bigger than the room they're standing in.

Five things I'm looking for

The one the group follows

Five friends, one decision-maker. Watch who the others check with, who orders first, who tells the story while the rest listen.

Tell: everyone's eyes go to them

Too sharp for the room

People skills and presence way beyond the job they're doing. There's no way that role pays what they're worth.

Tell: "you don't belong here"

Service they weren't paid for

Remembers your name, fixes a problem nobody asked them to fix, hustles when the manager isn't looking. That's character showing up for free.

Tell: extra effort, no audience

Carriage and eye contact

Head up, steady eyes, moves with intent. Ambition has a posture, and you can read it across a room.

Tell: they take up their space

Real warmth

Smiles at strangers, easy with people, energy that lifts the counter instead of draining it. Coachable people are usually warm people.

Tell: you liked them in 10 seconds

Get clear or you'll clone yourself

If you don't know what you're looking for, you default to recruiting whoever will say yes. Decide before you leave the house.

Standard, not desperation

Where the talent already is

A gym people pay extra for

They're spending three times more because they take something in their life seriously. Serious is the trait; fitness is just where it's showing.

Local coffee shops, weekday mornings

Working people fueling up before a shift or on lunch. Sit with a book, keep your head up, let the room come to you.

Airports and planes

Travel costs money. Briefcases, laptops, career people with time to talk and nowhere to go for two hours.

Networking events outside our industry

Skip the finance rooms. Go where real estate, health, fitness, and trades people gather — you're the only one of you there.

Your regular spots

Same restaurant, same barber, same counter. Tip well, learn names, be a bright spot. Three visits in, they ask what you do — and you didn't have to open.

Anywhere, honestly

Stop waiting for perfect timing and the perfect setting. It doesn't exist. Everyone is busy. Good recruiters find the opening in ordinary moments.

The honest-compliment rule. If you can't name one true thing you like about them, don't prospect them. Fake compliments about shoes they hate is exactly why people think this is cheesy. Genuine or nothing.
Stage 3

Conversation: tone carries it, not wording

Here's the biggest secret in the whole guide: greet a total stranger in the same tone you'd use for a friend you're happy to run into. Same words, that tone — it lands genuine every time.

"Do you mind if I ask what you do?" said flat is a survey. Said warm, it's a compliment.

Three moves, in order

  1. Compliment — something trueName the thing you actually noticed. Specific beats clever. Ten words, max.
  2. Curiosity — one honest questionAsk about them and then shut up and listen. Nod, react, follow the thread they give you. You're not steering yet.
  3. Connect — leave with a way to reach themYou're not pitching, presenting, or closing. You're exchanging contact info like two people who enjoyed a conversation.

Openers that work — steal these

You probably get asked this all the time — do you mind if I ask what you do for work?Universal. Works anywhere, on anybody.
I have to ask — how'd you end up working here? You're way sharper than this job pays for.Retail, restaurant, front counter. Say it warm, not slick.
You've got great energy. Are you in business, or is this the day job?Great for someone with real customer-service presence.
You're clearly consistent in here — do you mind if I ask what you do outside the gym?Gym. Compliment the discipline, not the body.
Watching your group for a minute, it's pretty obvious you're the one running the show. What do you do?The leader in a friend group. They'll smile — nobody ever tells them.
You handled that better than most people twice your pay grade. Who taught you that?After you watch them fix a problem or handle a rude customer.
Do you see yourself doing this five years from now, or is something else the plan?Use after the small talk is already flowing.

Don't memorize seven. Pick two, run them a hundred times, make them yours.

Stage 3.5

When they flip it back on you

Party, wedding, gym floor, backyard barbecue — sooner or later they ask what you do. This is where most people lose the room: they get excited and explain the whole business standing up. Don't. Your answer is a door, not a presentation.

Answer short. Hand it back. Every answer ends with a question.

The answer ladder — only climb when they make you

Start on rung one. You only go to rung two if they ask again, and rung three if they ask a third time. Their curiosity pulls the information out of you — that's what makes them lean in instead of back up.

Rung 1
I have a business. What do you do?Five words, then it's back on them. Most conversations never leave this rung — and that's fine.
Rung 2
they asked again
I have an agency where I get people licensed and trained to do complimentary financial plans for businesses and families. What do you do exactly?Notice you described the agency, not the products. Then straight back to them.
Rung 3
still asking
We help them save on taxes, build wealth — anything to do with money.Broad on purpose. Specific answers invite debate; broad answers invite questions.
Then stop
How long have you been doing that type of work?Say it and shut up. The silence after your answer is the tool. Do not fill it, do not add a sentence, do not explain. Let them talk.

If they say "like what?"

Most people are paying more in taxes than they need to, or they've got life insurance that's working against them. We look at the full picture and fix whatever's broken.One sentence of problem, one sentence of solution. Then stop again.

When they're actually interested — two invites

Honestly, it's easier to show you than explain it. We do a free 20 to 30-minute financial review — what does your schedule look like next week?Use this when it's personal and they're leaning in.
My company's hosting a free Zoom this week — Thursday at 6PM or Saturday at 9AM Pacific. It covers the financial stuff most of us were never taught. Practical, eye-opening, and most people walk away wishing they'd learned it years ago. I'd genuinely like you there — which works better, Thursday or Saturday?Lower commitment than a sit-down, and the two-option close means the answer is a day, not a yes or no.

Either way, still get the contact before you walk away. An invite they don't confirm is worth nothing without a number.

The discipline here is silence. Every one of these lines is built to end and leave a gap. New people fill the gap with nervous talking and give away the whole presentation in a driveway. If you say your line and it feels uncomfortably quiet — you did it right.
Field notes

How it actually sounds in the wild

These are real moments, real words, real results — including the ones that went nowhere. Read the outcomes carefully: most of these people didn't stay in the business, and every one of them was still worth the conversation. That's the point.

The drive-thru windowToo sharp for the room

Pulls up to a McDonald's window. The woman working it is polished, well-spoken, clearly overqualified for the headset.

You're way too sharp to be working at McDonald's. She said people probably think she hears that constantly — nobody ever actually says it. So why are you here? "I just needed money. It was the first place that hired me." Let me get your contact info.

Why it works He named the gap between her and her job out loud. People working overlooked positions almost never get told they're bigger than the role.

What happened Recruited her later. She didn't stick — and it was still a rep worth taking, at a window he was sitting at anyway.

The coffee shop, with a bookWeekday morning

Sat in a local coffee shop with The Magic of Thinking Big open in front of him. Barely read a page — he watched the door. A sharp-looking couple walks in and asks about the book.

Hey, do you mind if I ask you a question — what do you do for work? You look sharp.

Why it works The book gave them a reason to open him, so his first line landed mid-conversation instead of cold. Sitting somewhere on purpose beats walking around hunting.

What happened Traded info, recruited them roughly six weeks later, made about six figures training them. They didn't stay in the business — they're still friends today.

The restaurant he goes to every weekThey opened him

Same spot near his house. Walks in, they know his order, he always tips well. No pitch, ever — just a good customer with good energy. Yesterday the server asked him.

"Can I ask what you do, bro? What do you do?" Five-minute conversation, got his social media.

Why it works Presence over time did the prospecting. When you show up confident and generous in the same place repeatedly, people ask on their own — and you never had to open.

What happened He'll be the next direct. Cost: zero courage, because the door opened from their side.

The gymCompliment the discipline

Upscale gym, mid-workout, a guy who obviously trains seriously.

Excuse me brother, can I ask you a question? You're in great shape — obviously you train a lot. What do you do for work when you're not in here? "I'm in law enforcement." Okay, got it.

Why it works He complimented the consistency, not the body, then asked one honest question. Same room he was going to be in regardless.

What happened His number one guy in the company came out of a gym conversation. So did his top couple.

A server, mid-shiftShort and human

Waiting on a table turn, thirty seconds of the server's attention.

Can I ask you a question — why do you work here? How'd you end up here? You remind me of someone I work with. How long have you been here? I can tell you love talking to people. Do you want to be working here for the rest of your career?

Why it works Every one of these is short enough to fit in a shift and open-ended enough that the answer tells you whether to keep going.

What happened This is his everyday rhythm — he isn't setting aside time to prospect anymore, he prospects as he moves through his day.

The leader in a friend groupThe direct invite

Watched a group for about twenty minutes. One person is clearly running the table — the others follow their lead.

I watched you the last twenty minutes — the way you interact, the way your whole group follows you. If you were in business with me, I have no doubt you'd make six figures. What do you do now? Are you open to learning something new, even part-time? Let me get your contact info.

Why it works The compliment is evidence-based — he says what he observed, which is impossible to dismiss as flattery. Then the ask is small: open to learning, not ready to join.

What happened Some he invites directly like this. Most he just connects with on social and recruits weeks, months, or years later.

The low-pressure close he uses everywhereMemorize this one

The exit line, whenever the conversation has warmth but no time to go deeper.

You want to work this job for twenty-five bucks an hour the rest of your life? I might have a shot for you. Let me get your contact — I'll have someone call you when I get back to the office. I can't guarantee you anything, but it's worth a conversation.

Why it works "I can't guarantee you anything" removes every ounce of sales pressure and raises your status at the same time. You're not begging — you're offering one conversation.

What happened This is the line that's been in circulation for 13 years for a reason. Say it warm, say it like you're doing them a favor, because you are.

The counter-example — don't do this. "Hey, I really like your shoes" when they're wearing beat-up sneakers they hate. They know you don't mean it, and now you're the cheesy guy. If you can't name something you actually noticed, walk on by. There's another person thirty feet away.
Stage 4

The contact: how you actually leave with it

This is where most people fold — good conversation, warm goodbye, no way to ever talk again. Ask for the contact out loud, every time. Two ways to do it.

Option A · The soft connect (use this 80% of the time)

Hey, I enjoyed this — are you on Instagram? Let's connect.Zero pressure, nearly always yes. Follow them right there while you're standing together.
I've got to run, but let me get your number so I'm not that guy who says "we should talk" and disappears.Honest and a little funny. People respect it.

Then you do nothing salesy. You reply to a story, congratulate a win, be a normal friend. Two weeks or two years later, the door opens on its own — and you're already in.

Option B · The direct invite (when the talent is loud)

I'll be straight with you — I've watched you the last twenty minutes and you've got something. I build people in the financial space and I'm always looking for talent. Are you open to hearing about something, even part-time?Then wait. Let the silence sit.
Give me your number and I'll have someone reach out. I can't promise you anything — but it's worth one conversation.Honest, low pressure, high status. Never oversell the ask.

When it doesn't land

"No thanks" costs you nothing and it isn't about you. Say "totally fair — good luck with everything," smile, and go. Locked doors unlock later, and the ones who watched you handle a no gracefully are often the ones who come back. The rep still counts. Log it.

Same day, before bed: name into your phone or the CRM, where you met, one true detail about them, and one thing to say next time. A contact you can't remember anything about is a dead contact.
Where this guide ends. The second they agree to sit down with you — a Zoom, a coffee, a kitchen table — this guide is finished and you pick up The Sit-Down (sit-down.html). Different room, different job. Nothing on this page is meant to close anybody, and you should never try.

Leader's page

How to teach this in 30 minutes

Don't read the guide to them. Teach three ideas, make them do it in the room, send them out with a number to hit. Talking about courage doesn't build courage — reps do.

  1. 0–5 min Tell on yourselfOpen with your own story of a conversation you were too scared to start, or one you started that changed something. Real beats polished. This is what gives them permission.
  2. 5–12 min Teach the three ideas only(1) This is CEO work — CEOs find talent. (2) Fear is ego; it's five seconds. (3) It's not what you say, it's what you see. Nothing else. Three ideas stick; twelve don't.
  3. 12–20 min Spot-the-talent drillPairs. Each person describes a real stranger they saw this week — barista, server, gym, gate agent — and names which of the five signals they showed. This trains eyes, which is the part nobody practices.
  4. 20–27 min Opener reps out loudStanding, in threes: prospect, prospector, coach. Each person runs their two openers three times. Coach only gives feedback on tone and posture — never wording. Everyone rotates.
  5. 27–30 min Assign the number and closeTen conversations, five contacts before next meeting. Everybody says their number out loud. Then send them out.

The team challenge

"Walk out with momentum." Every gym trip, coffee run, and errand this week = one conversation. Post the win in the chat — the story, not the outcome.

Celebrate the reps, not the yeses

Shout out the person who got told no three times and kept going. What you applaud is what duplicates. Applaud outcomes and the scared ones quit.

Read one field note out loud

Pick a different one each week and read it word for word — then ask the room, "what did he notice before he said anything?" The examples teach the eyes faster than the theory does.

Go first, in front of them

Prospect somebody live with a new associate standing next to you. One live rep from you is worth an hour of training.

Ask one question in every 1-on-1

"How many conversations did you start?" Not how many signed. Coach the input and the confidence, and the output follows.
